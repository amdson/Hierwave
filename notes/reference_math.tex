\noindent The models, the sampler, and the training algorithm, as equations.
Definitions only; the arguments are in \code{history/channels.tex} and the
per-experiment notes. Section numbers follow \code{reference.md}.

The setting: a world is an $H \times W$ grid of \emph{tiles} (voxels in 3D) described at
several \emph{levels} of resolution. A level is a block size $h \in \{1, 2, 4, \dots\}$; level
$h$ partitions the tile grid into $h \times h$ blocks and holds one value per block. The
levels together form a Markov random field (MRF) whose factors are small tables; generation
runs top-down, each level sampled with the coarser ones held fixed.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Objects}

\paragraph{Channels and views.} A channel $c$ at block size $h_c$ holds a state
$z_c : \Lambda_c \to \Dom_c = \{0, \dots, D_c - 1\}$ on the grid
$\Lambda_c = \{0, \dots, H/h_c - 1\} \times \{0, \dots, W/h_c - 1\}$; $D_c$ is its number of
values. The tile channel has $h = 1$. A \emph{view} of $c$ is a map
$\alpha : \Dom_c \to \{0, \dots, V_\alpha - 1\}$ onto a small feature; factors read views,
never raw values. A \emph{fixed} mask $\phi_c \subseteq \Lambda_c$ marks cells the sampler
never updates.

\paragraph{Parent cell and block.} For $p \in \Lambda_a$ and a coarser-or-equal channel $b$
($h_a \le h_b$), the cell of $b$ containing $p$, shifted by an offset $\delta$ in $b$'s cells, is
\begin{equation}
  q_b(p, \delta) = \left\lfloor \frac{p\, h_a}{h_b} \right\rfloor + \delta, \qquad \delta \in \Z^2 ;
\end{equation}
conversely $\mathrm{block}(q) = \{p \in \Lambda_a : q_b(p, 0) = q\}$ is the set of $a$-cells
inside the $b$-cell $q$.

\paragraph{Factors.} A factor is anything that assigns each candidate value at a cell a local
energy. Three kinds exist: tables (this paragraph), the conv potential (Section~\ref{sec:convpot}),
and the certificate (below), whose energy is computed by a procedure. Tables are the fast path
(a candidate's energy is a lookup) and are also what training produces. A table factor is a table
$T$ in energy units (nats), with $+\infty$ for hard rules, indexed by views of one or two
channels. Writing $a$ for the finer (or only) channel and $b$ for the other, the three kinds are
\begin{align}
  \text{pair:}\quad & E^{\mathrm{pair}}_{a\alpha, b\beta, \delta}(z) = \sum_{p \in \Lambda_a}
    T\big[\alpha(z_a(p)),\ \beta^{\mathrm{pad}}(z_b(q_b(p,\delta)))\big], \\
  \text{count:}\quad & E^{\mathrm{count}}_{a\alpha, b\beta}(z) = \sum_{q \in \Lambda_b}
    T\Big[\beta(z_b(q)),\ \textstyle\sum_{p \in \mathrm{block}(q)} \alpha(z_a(p))\Big], \qquad h_a < h_b, \\
  \text{unary:}\quad & E^{\mathrm{un}}_{a\alpha}(z) = \sum_{p \in \Lambda_a} T\big[\alpha(z_a(p))\big].
\end{align}
A pair factor is one offset $\delta$ applied at every cell of $a$, i.e.\ a translation-invariant
term with a position-free table; a neighbourhood is a set of such factors, one per offset, and
the largest $|\delta|$ among a channel's factors sets its sampler's radius. With $b = a$ and
$\delta \ne 0$ it is a same-level neighbour term; with $h_b > h_a$ it is a parent read (the
parent cell, or a parent cell $\delta$ away). $\beta^{\mathrm{pad}}$ returns the factor's pad value when $q_b(p, \delta)$ is off
the grid, and the term is dropped when the pad is $-1$. A factor is \emph{homed} on its finer side
$a$. The sampler of a channel reads only the factors homed on it, plus the reflections of
same-level pairs (transposed table, negated offset), so during generation a coarse channel never
sees a finer one (top-down).

\paragraph{Honour factors.} A factor is \emph{honour} when it makes a finer channel agree with
a view of a coarser one, i.e.\ a pair factor whose table penalises $\alpha(z_a(p)) \ne
\beta(z_b(q_b(p,0)))$: hard ($+\infty$) for guarantees, soft (a cost $\kappa$ per violated cell)
otherwise. A coarse value is \emph{honourable} if some finer completion has finite energy.

\paragraph{Certificate.} The factor that enforces connectivity or support. It lives
on a tile channel with three views, $m = \mathrm{mass}$ ($0$: not part of the structure),
$\tau = \mathrm{trunk}$ ($1$: a root of the structure), and \emph{join bits}
$J_d(t, t') \in \{0,1\}$ saying whether tile value $t$ joins value $t'$ across side
$d \in \{\mathrm{N}, \mathrm{E}, \mathrm{S}, \mathrm{W}\}$; and on a companion distance channel
$d : \Lambda \to \{0, \dots, D_{\max}, \mathrm{INF}\}$ at the same level, $\mathrm{INF} = D_{\max} + 1$.
Write $t_p = z(p)$, $q \sim_d p$ for the 4-neighbour of $p$ across side $d$. Cell $p$ is
\emph{valid} iff
\begin{equation}
  \big(m_p = 0 \wedge d_p = \mathrm{INF}\big) \ \vee\
  \big(\tau_p = 1 \wedge d_p = 0\big) \ \vee\
  \Big(d_p < \mathrm{INF} \wedge \exists\, q \sim_d p:\ J_d(t_p, t_q) \wedge m_q \ge m_p \wedge d_q < d_p\Big).
\end{equation}
A neighbour $q$ satisfying the last clause is a \emph{witness} for $p$; $p$'s \emph{dependants}
are the neighbours for which $p$ is the only witness. The energy is $+\infty$ on an invalid cell
and $\gamma\, d_p$ otherwise ($\gamma$ is \code{delta} in the code). With the \code{tree} option,
exactly one neighbour may witness $p$ (its parent) and every other joined neighbour must be a
child ($m_q \le m_p$, $d_q > d_p$), so the join graph is a forest.

\paragraph{Painted channels.} A \emph{painter} $\pi_c : \Dom_c^{\Lambda_c} \to \Dom_w^{\Lambda_w}$,
$h_w < h_c$, writes a finer channel $w$ deterministically from $z_c$; $w$ is fixed everywhere and
is rewritten whenever $z_c$ changes. Honour factors then read $w$ through position-free pair
tables; the dependence on where a cell sits inside its block (which tiles of a block are in an
object's disc, say) lives in $\pi_c$. The \emph{spill} of a painter is the set of $w$-cells
outside the blocks of a region that the painter writes from values inside it.

\paragraph{Promotion.} A hard factor on a view $\alpha$ of a channel $u$ with many values (an
exemplar coordinate, say) rejects most of $u$'s candidates and can leave no candidate at all;
promotion moves the hard factor onto a small channel that is updated exactly, and lets $u$ pay
only a soft cost for disagreeing with it. Add a channel $w$ at the same level with
$\Dom_w = \mathrm{range}(\alpha)$, move the factors that read $\alpha(z_u)$ to read $w$, and add
the cell-wise factor
\begin{equation}
  E^{\mathrm{cell}}(w_p, u_p) = \mathrm{cost}\big(w_p, \alpha(u_p)\big),
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{cost}$ one of: $\nu\,\1[w \ne \alpha(u)]$ (a soft copy, which tempers the hard
factors that now read $w$); $-\log \hat p(w \mid u)$ counted from an exemplar; or the induced free
energy of Section~\ref{sec:identity}. Marginalising $w$ recovers the original model with its hard
factors softened by exactly $\mathrm{cost}$.

\section{The two models}
\label{sec:identity}

Index the levels $\ell = L, \dots, 1$ from coarsest to finest, block sizes $h_L > \dots > h_1 = 1$.
Let $z_\ell$ be the channels at level $\ell$, $z_{>\ell}$ the coarser ones, $z_{\ge \ell}$ both,
and $E_\ell(z_\ell \mid z_{>\ell})$ the sum of the factors homed on level $\ell$ (they read
level $\ell$ and coarser levels only).

\paragraph{Bidirectional (the ideal).} The undirected joint of every declared factor,
\begin{equation}
  \pstar(z) = \frac{1}{Z}\exp\Big(-\sum_\ell E_\ell(z_\ell \mid z_{>\ell})\Big).
\end{equation}
It is the specification; it is never sampled in production.

\paragraph{Forward (the chain, what ships).}
\begin{equation}
  q(z) = \prod_{\ell = L}^{1} q_\ell(z_\ell \mid z_{>\ell}),
\end{equation}
where $q_\ell$ is the law of level $\ell$ after $S_\ell$ sweeps of a kernel that leaves
$\exp(-(E_\ell + \Phi_\ell))$ invariant (Gibbs for small domains, candidate-set Gibbs for large
ones; Section~\ref{sec:sampler}) at temperature $T = 1$, started from the \emph{painted
initialisation} (a consistent refinement of $z_{>\ell}$ written by the painters), with
$z_{>\ell}$ clamped. $\Phi_\ell(z_\ell \mid z_{>\ell})$ is the \emph{learned potential} of
level $\ell$. We call $S_\ell$ sweeps of this kernel the \emph{budgeted kernel}.

\paragraph{The identity.} Define the free energy of the levels below $\ell$, recursively from
the bottom,
\begin{equation}
  F_{\ell}(z_{> \ell}) \;=\; -\log \sum_{z_\ell} \exp\!\Big(-E_\ell(z_\ell \mid z_{>\ell}) - F_{\ell-1}(z_{\ge \ell})\Big),
  \qquad F_{0} \equiv 0 .
\end{equation}
Then $\pstar(z_{\ge \ell}) \propto \exp\big(-\sum_{\ell' \ge \ell} E_{\ell'} - F_{\ell-1}(z_{\ge \ell})\big)$,
and the chain with equilibrated kernels reproduces $\pstar$ exactly iff
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_\ell = F_{\ell-1} \quad \text{for every level } \ell .
\end{equation}
The learned potential of a level is the free energy the levels below induce on it. With the
budgeted kernel in place of the exact sum in the definition of $F$, the identity holds for the
chain \emph{as it runs} (the free energy as that kernel realises it at that budget).

\paragraph{Cluster expansion.} $F_{\ell-1}$ is approximated by local terms over the cells of
level $\ell$,
\begin{equation}
  F_{\ell-1}(z_\ell) \approx \sum_{p} f_1(z_p) + \sum_{\langle p, q\rangle} f_2(z_p, z_q) + \cdots ,
\end{equation}
exact in the limit where the correlation length of the finer levels is below the block size
(off-critical content); the residual of the fit (Section~\ref{sec:train}) measures the omitted
orders.

\paragraph{Support and finite part.} The support of $\Phi_\ell$ ($+\infty$ on coarse values
that are not honourable) comes from honourability checks, not from the fit; only the finite part
is learned.

\section{The sampler}
\label{sec:sampler}

A sweep of a channel at level $\ell$ visits every non-fixed site $p$ once. At $p$, with
everything else fixed, the candidate energy $e_p(t)$ is the sum of the factors homed on the
channel that involve $p$, evaluated with $z_p = t$: the designed terms of $E_\ell$ (reading
level $\ell$ and coarser) and the tables of $\Phi_\ell$, which is a same-level potential and
enters as ordinary unary and pair rows. Factors homed on finer levels are never read; $\Phi_\ell$
stands in for them. The site draws its exact conditional,
\begin{equation}
  t \sim \frac{\exp(-e_p(t)/T)}{\sum_{t'} \exp(-e_p(t')/T)}
  \quad\text{as}\quad
  \arg\max_{t:\, e_p(t) < \infty} \big[-e_p(t)/T + G_t\big],\ G_t \sim \mathrm{Gumbel}(0,1);
\end{equation}
a site with no finite candidate keeps its value. Sites are visited colour by colour so that sites
of one colour do not interact: $(x+y) \bmod 2$ when every factor has radius 1, $(x + 2y) \bmod 5$
at radius 2. With a certificate the draw is joint in $(t, d)$: for each $t$ the valid $d$ form an
interval $[a_t, b_t)$ ($a_t$ one above the smallest witness $d$, $b_t$ the smallest $d$ among
$p$'s dependants, which must stay valid), so
\begin{equation}
  w_t = \exp(-e_p(t)/T) \sum_{d = a_t}^{b_t - 1} e^{-\gamma d / T}
      = \exp(-e_p(t)/T)\, e^{-\gamma a_t/T}\, \frac{1 - e^{-\gamma (b_t - a_t)/T}}{1 - e^{-\gamma/T}},
\end{equation}
$t$ by Gumbel-max over $\log w_t$, then $d$ from the truncated geometric law on $[a_t, b_t)$.
Dependants read their other neighbours, hence radius 2.

\paragraph{Large domains.} Enumerating $\Dom_c$ at every site is only for small $D_c$. A channel
with a large domain (an exemplar coordinate, $D_c$ = the exemplar's cells) draws over a
\emph{candidate set} $C_p \subset \Dom_c$ instead: the current value, the coherent continuation
$z_q + (p - q)$ of each neighbour $q$, $K$ random values, and $K_t$ random values consistent
with the tile under $p$. The draw is the same Gumbel-max restricted to $C_p$. Because $C_p$
contains the current value and is chosen without reference to $z_p$, the step leaves the
level's conditional invariant, which is all the chain needs. Cost per site: $|C_p|$ energy
evaluations, each over the candidate's footprint; $\Phi_\ell$ is then evaluated on the fly on that
footprint rather than read from tables. Any such invariant kernel may stand in for the Gibbs
sweep in $q_\ell$ and in the identity of Section~\ref{sec:identity}.

\section{The training (combining) algorithm}
\label{sec:train}

Input: a coarse channel $c$ at level $\ell$; the levels below it, already corrected (carrying
$E + \Phi$); its painter $\pi$; and a \emph{reference value} $r \in \Dom_c$, the background
value (empty, absent) against which every other value is measured. Output: $\Phi_\ell$ as tables
for the forward kernel. Nothing samples $c$ itself.

\paragraph{Windows.} A window $W \subset \Lambda_c$ (e.g.\ $3 \times 3$ cells) with halo
$\partial W$ (the one-cell ring around it). Draw $z_W$ and $z_{\partial W}$ from a proposal
(uniform at first, later windows cut from forward samples); set $c$ to $r$ outside
$W \cup \partial W$. Let $z^{r}_W$ be the configuration with $r$ on all of $W$ and the same
halo. The fine region $R(W)$ is the blocks of $W \cup \partial W$ plus the painters' spill.

\paragraph{Free energy per window.} With $E_{\mathrm{fine}}$ the energy of the levels below
$c$ restricted to $R(W)$ (the coarse values clamped),
\begin{equation}
  F(z_W \mid z_{\partial W}) = -\log \sum_{z_{R(W)}} \exp\big(-E_{\mathrm{fine}}(z_{R(W)} \mid z_W, z_{\partial W})\big),
  \qquad
  y(W) = F(z_W \mid z_{\partial W}) - F(z^{r}_W \mid z_{\partial W}).
\end{equation}
$F$ is computed exactly where the toy allows it (transfer matrix; per-cell products when the fine
cells are independent given the paint), otherwise by annealed importance sampling (AIS) with the
forward model's own fine kernel. For AIS split $E_{\mathrm{fine}} = E_{\mathrm{un}} +
E_{\mathrm{rest}}$ (the unary factors of the fine channel vs.\ everything else), replace
$+\infty$ by a finite $\Lambda$ ($30$) in $E_{\mathrm{rest}}$, and take inverse temperatures
$\beta_0 = 10^{-3} < \beta_1 < \dots < \beta_K = 1$ (linear). With $E_{\mathrm{un}, s}(t)$ the
unary energy of value $t$ at fine site $s$,
\begin{align}
  \log Z_0 &= \sum_{s \in R(W)} \log \sum_{t} e^{-E_{\mathrm{un}, s}(t)}, \\
  x_0 &\sim p_0 \propto e^{-E_{\mathrm{un}}}, \qquad
  x_k \sim \text{one Gibbs sweep of } p_{\beta_k} \propto e^{-E_{\mathrm{un}} - \beta_k E_{\mathrm{rest}}} \text{ from } x_{k-1}, \\
  \log w &= -\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} (\beta_{k+1} - \beta_k)\, E_{\mathrm{rest}}(x_k), \\
  \widehat{\log Z} &= \log Z_0 + \logmeanexp_{m = 1..M}\, \log w^{(m)}, \qquad \hat F = -\widehat{\log Z},
\end{align}
over $M$ independent chains. $\hat F$ is biased upward at small $K$; $K = 256$, $M = 4$--$8$ was
adequate on the toys.

\paragraph{Paint potential.} The parametrisation of $\Phi_\ell$ that generalises to unseen
value pairs: a local energy over the painted image rather than over $c$'s values. Let
$\pi = \pi(z_c)$ be the painted channel at fine resolution with values $\{0, \dots, V-1\}$,
indexed by fine cells $t$, and $\mathcal{O} = \{(0,1), (1,0), (1,1), (1,-1)\}$ the four
independent offsets of the 8-neighbourhood. Then
\begin{equation}
  F_\theta(\pi) = \sum_{t} u\big(\pi_t\big) + \sum_{\delta \in \mathcal{O}} \sum_{t} g_\delta\big(\pi_t, \pi_{t+\delta}\big),
  \qquad \theta = (u, \{g_\delta\}),
\end{equation}
off-grid partners dropped. $F_\theta = N(\pi) \cdot \theta$ is linear in $\theta$ through the
count features $N(\pi) \in \R^{V + |\mathcal{O}| V^2}$ (how often each value, and each offset
pair of values, occurs). Regression with the halo painted:
\begin{equation}
  x(W) = N\big(\pi(z_W, z_{\partial W})\big) - N\big(\pi(z^{r}_W, z_{\partial W})\big), \qquad
  \hat\theta = \arg\min_\theta \sum_W \big(x(W)\!\cdot\!\theta - y(W)\big)^2 + \lambda \|\theta\|^2 .
\end{equation}
The halo's own terms cancel in $x$ and $y$; the window--halo cross terms are features. The
relative residual $\sqrt{\sum_W (x\cdot\hat\theta - y)^2 / \sum_W y^2}$ measures what is not local in
the painted map at this order, down to the AIS noise floor.

\paragraph{Materialisation.} Tables over $\Dom_c$ for the forward kernel. Write $\pi(v)$ for
the paint of a configuration with $v$ at one cell and $r$ everywhere else, and
$\pi(v \text{ at } p, v' \text{ at } p + d)$ likewise for a pair. Then
\begin{align}
  u_c(v) &= F_{\hat\theta}\big(\pi(v)\big) - F_{\hat\theta}\big(\pi(r)\big), \\
  g_{c,d}(v, v') &= F_{\hat\theta}\big(\pi(v \text{ at } p,\ v' \text{ at } p + d)\big) - u_c(v) - u_c(v') - F_{\hat\theta}\big(\pi(r)\big),
\end{align}
for the coarse adjacencies $d$ (east, south, the two diagonals). Exact for a linear $F_\theta$.
For large $D_c$, skip the tables and evaluate $F_{\hat\theta}$ on the candidate's painted
footprint at sampling time. Upward recursion: for the next coarser channel, $c$ with
$E_c + \Phi_c$ is the fine level, and the AIS base $p_0$ is built from $c$'s unaries (designed
and learned $u_c$).

\paragraph{Gauge.} Only $y$ is observed, so $u$ and $\{g_\delta\}$ are identified up to the
transformations that leave every window sum invariant (e.g.\ moving a constant from $u$ into
$g_\delta$). The materialised coarse tables are invariant and are what is used. When comparing
coarse tables between methods, fix the reference gauge ($g_c(r, \cdot) = g_c(\cdot, r) = 0$) or
double-centre (subtract row and column means).

\section{The conv potential (coarse-level pair potentials)}
\label{sec:convpot}

A parametric factor over one channel, kept for coarse levels. Each value has a feature vector
$e : \Dom \to \R^k$, the channel's \emph{feature set}, fixed before fitting: the rows of a free
matrix $M \in \R^{D \times k}$ (one-hot, or learned like token embeddings), or derived from what
the value asks of the finer level (the \emph{stamp}: paint $v$ alone in its block's frame and take
the indicator over footprint cells and paint values). Offsets $d \in \{-1,0,1\}^2$ index the
$3 \times 3$ neighbourhood. Then
\begin{equation}
  E(z) = \sum_p \Big[\, a \cdot e(z_p) + \sum_{d \ne 0} e(z_p)^{\!\top} A_d\, e(z_{p+d})
       + v \cdot \softplus\!\Big(\sum_{d} W_d\, e(z_{p+d}) + b\Big)\Big],
\end{equation}
with $a \in \R^k$, $A_d \in \R^{k\times k}$, $W_d \in \R^{m \times k}$, $b, v \in \R^m$; off-grid
neighbours use $e(\mathrm{pad})$ or drop out. The softplus term with $m$ hidden units (the
\emph{plaquette head}) adds 3- and 4-body interactions. With one-hot $M$ and $m = 0$ this is the
8-offset pair-table model and the fit (same regression as above) is closed-form ridge; for $m > 0$
it is fitted by Adam. With stamp features and $A_d$ tied to the geometry (entry $(s, s')$ of $A_d$ is
$g_\delta$ for cell pairs within the stencil offsets $\mathcal{O}$, zero otherwise) the free
parameters are the stencil $(u, \{g_\delta\})$ and the factor is the paint potential of
Section~\ref{sec:train} written over coarse values; its materialised tables are exact.
Candidate cost in the kernel: $9k$ operations (bilinear) $+\, 9km$ (head, updated incrementally),
or table lookups when materialised. On the toys the head added nothing measurable; the bilinear
form with one-hot or learned features reached the estimator's noise floor at the top level but
did not generalise to held-out value pairs at the mid level; stamp features did.

\section{Self-play moment matching (retained as a diagnostic)}

The earlier trainer. The learned tables are exponential-family parameters $\theta_k$, one per
table entry, with sufficient statistics $N_k(z)$ (how often entry $k$ occurs in $z$). For a
forward sample $z \sim q$ and a one-site kernel that leaves $\pstar$ invariant (the full
$\pstar$ conditional at a site, including factors homed below it),
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_k(z) = \sum_{p} \sum_{t} \pstar(z_p = t \mid z_{-p})\,\big[N_k(z \text{ with } z_p = t) - N_k(z)\big],
  \qquad
  \theta_k \leftarrow \theta_k - \eta\,\mathbb{E}_{q}[\Delta_k] ,
\end{equation}
the Rao-Blackwellised expected change of the statistics under one step of that kernel.
Contrastive divergence (CD-$K$) replaces the bracket by $N_k(z_K) - N_k(z) + \Delta_k(z_K)$
after $K$ sweeps of the $\pstar$ kernel. Fixed point: $q$ invariant under the kernel on the
features. Under hard honour the $\pstar$ conditional of a coarse site is a point mass (its
children pin it), so $\Delta \equiv 0$ and nothing is learned; this is why the free-energy route
replaced it.

\section{The toys' exact quantities}

\paragraph{Circles.} Tiles are $\mathrm{air}$ or $\mathrm{dirt}$, with a field $\mu$ (unary cost
$\mu$ per air tile). The level above (\emph{objects}, blocks of 8) holds either \emph{absent} or
a disc at one of 16 offsets inside the block; a present object demands dirt on its 13-cell disc
and air on the surrounding ring, at a soft-honour cost $\kappa$ per violated tile. The painter
writes each tile's \emph{demand} $\pi_t \in \{\mathrm{free}, \mathrm{dirt}, \mathrm{air}, \mathrm{conflict}\}$
(conflict: in one object's disc and another's ring). Given the objects, tiles are independent,
with per-tile partition functions
\begin{equation}
  Z_{\mathrm{free}} = 1 + e^{-\mu},\quad
  Z_{\mathrm{dirt}} = 1 + e^{-\mu-\kappa},\quad
  Z_{\mathrm{air}, n} = e^{-\mu} + e^{-n\kappa},\quad
  Z_{\mathrm{conflict}} = e^{-\kappa} + e^{-\mu-\kappa},
\end{equation}
where $n$ is the number of rings covering an air-demand tile. So $F(\text{objects}) = -\sum_t
\log Z_{\pi_t}$; a conflict tile costs
$-\log Z_{\mathrm{conflict}} + \log Z_{\mathrm{dirt}} + \log Z_{\mathrm{air},1} - \log Z_{\mathrm{free}}$
and a ring tile shared by two objects gains
$-\log Z_{\mathrm{air},2} + 2 \log Z_{\mathrm{air},1} - \log Z_{\mathrm{free}}$,
relative to the objects placed separately. The induced potential on the object level is exactly
a unary paint potential ($g_\delta \equiv 0$). An isolated object costs
$F_{\mathrm{obj}} = F(\text{one object}) - F(\text{none})$; the designed \emph{presence bonus}
(unary $-b$ on every present value) with $b = F_{\mathrm{obj}} - \log 16$ makes an isolated
block present or absent with equal probability.

\paragraph{Potts.} Tiles carry $q$ colours with cyclic distance $\rho(c, c') = \min(|c - c'|,
q - |c - c'|)$ and a neighbour coupling $J\,\rho$; the level above (\emph{mid}, blocks of $B$)
holds a block colour $m$ with honour cost $\kappa\,\1[c \ne m]$ per tile; the top level holds a
palette (a pair of adjacent colours) with honour cost $\lambda$ per mid cell outside it. At
$\kappa \to \infty$ the induced coupling between adjacent blocks is $g(m, m') = B\,J\,\rho(m, m')$
per shared edge of length $B$; the mid level is then a $q$-state clock model with coupling
$BJ$, ordered for $J \gtrsim 0.2$ at $B = 4$, $q = 4$. Block free energies are exact by a column
transfer matrix over $q^{B}$ states.
