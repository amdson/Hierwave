\section{Terms}
\label{sec:terms}

\paragraph{Lattice and levels.} The world is a grid of \emph{tiles} of side
$1$. A \emph{level} $h \in \{8, 1\}$ in the current code (any power of two in
principle) is the grid of cells of side $h$ tiles; cell $p$ at level $h$
covers the $h \times h$ tiles below it. Level $8$ cells are called
\emph{chunks}.

\paragraph{Channel.} A channel $c$ is a grid of \emph{variables} at one
level $h_c$, each taking a value in a finite \emph{domain} $\Dom_c =
\{0, \dots, D_c - 1\}$, together with its \emph{views}. The channel's state is
the array $z_c \in \Dom_c^{\text{cells}}$. A channel either has a
\emph{kernel} (it is sampled) or is \emph{designed} (its state is written
by a procedure that reads only coarser or designed channels).

\paragraph{View.} A view $\alpha$ of channel $c$ is a fixed table
$\alpha: \Dom_c \to \{0, \dots, V_\alpha - 1\}$, a lookup from a value to
something smaller. Views are declared with their width $V_\alpha$ and are
never sampled; they are the vocabulary factors are written in. Examples:
the solid bit of a tile, its mass, its port on the north side, the
exemplar statement at a coordinate.

\paragraph{Variable versus factor.} Variables carry state and are drawn;
factors carry energy and are summed. Nothing else exists at run time.
Promises, certificates, gates and refinements are choices of which
variables exist and which factors sit on them.

\paragraph{Factor.} A factor is a table of energies over views of one or
two variables at declared positions. Three kinds are implemented:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{pair}: $E[\alpha(z_a[p]),\ \beta(z_b[q])]$ with
  $q = \lfloor p\, h_a / h_b \rfloor + \delta$ in $b$'s cells. This covers
  same-level neighbours ($b = a$, $\delta = (0, 1)$) and parent reads
  ($b$ coarser) alike. A \emph{pad} gives $\beta$'s value when $q$ is off
  the grid, or says the term vanishes there.
\item \emph{count}: $E[\beta(z_b[q]),\ \sum_{p' \in \text{block}(q)}
  \alpha(z_a[p'])]$ with $b$ coarser than $a$: a table over a coarse value
  and the sum of a fine view inside its block.
\item \emph{unary}: $E[\alpha(z_a[p])]$.
\end{itemize}
A factor is \emph{hard} where its entries are $\infty$ and \emph{soft}
elsewhere; hard is not a separate type. A factor is \emph{homed} on its
finer (or equal) side; a coarser channel never reads a finer one
(top-down only).

\paragraph{Energy and target.} The energy of a state is the sum of all
factors over all positions, plus the certificate terms below; the target
is $\pi \propto e^{-E/T}$ at $T = 1$. Every kernel leaves $\pi$ invariant on
its block given the rest.

\paragraph{Kernel.} A kernel resamples one \emph{block} of variables from
its exact conditional given everything else. The generic kernel's block is
one variable at one site; it enumerates every value in the domain, sums
the factor rows touching the site, and draws by Gumbel-max over the finite
entries. A site with no finite candidate keeps its value and is counted.
A kernel owns its block's storage and consumes every factor touching it;
it knows nothing about which channel set wrote the factors.

\paragraph{Colouring.} Sites that share no factor may be updated together.
The compiler colours a channel's grid from the largest factor offset:
two colours at radius 1, five at radius 2 (the classes $(x + 2y) \bmod 5$),
one otherwise. The code runs colours in sequence; the colouring is the
statement of what could run in parallel.

\paragraph{Certificate.} The one \emph{computed} factor: a predicate on a
level's cells that cannot be written as a stored table because it reads a
cell, its neighbours, and a companion channel $d$ of integers. Each cell of
the tile channel carries a \emph{mass} view ($0$: not part of the
structure), a \emph{trunk} view (the structure's roots), and \emph{join}
bits (cell $p$ with value $t$ is joined to neighbour $q$ with value $t'$
across side $s$ iff $J_s[t, t']$). The companion $d_p \in \{0, \dots,
D_{\max}\} \cup \{\infty\}$ is a certificate of attachment. A cell is
\emph{valid} iff
\begin{itemize}
\item mass $0$ and $d = \infty$; or
\item a trunk and $d = 0$; or
\item $d < \infty$ and (\emph{plain mode}) some joined neighbour $q$ has
  $\text{mass}_q \ge \text{mass}_p$ and $d_q < d_p$; (\emph{tree mode})
  exactly one joined neighbour has $d_q < d_p$, with $\text{mass}_q \ge
  \text{mass}_p$ (the parent), and every other joined neighbour has
  $d_q > d_p$ and $\text{mass}_q \le \text{mass}_p$ (a child).
\end{itemize}
Validity is hard. A soft cost $\delta\, d_p$ per structure cell keeps $d$
small. Tree mode makes the join graph a forest with mass non-increasing
away from the trunks; plain mode allows extra joins and hence cycles.

\paragraph{Joint draw with a certificate.} The kernel draws $(t, d)$
together at a site. For each $t$ the valid $d$ form an interval:
above the smallest $d$ among witnesses, below the smallest $d$ among
\emph{dependants} (neighbours whose only possible parent is $p$). The sum
of $e^{-\delta d}$ over an interval is closed form, so $t$ is drawn by
Gumbel-max over the summed weights and then $d$ inside its interval. The
intervals mean a chain of $n$ cells contributes
$\prod_{i \le n} (e^{\delta i} - 1)^{-1}$ to the marginal of the tiles:
neutral while $\delta \cdot \text{depth} < 1$, so $\delta$ is small and
$D_{\max}$ must hold depth$/\delta$ ($0.05$ and $4096$ in use).

\paragraph{Derived channel, promotion, gate.} A \emph{derived channel} is
a sampled channel coupled to another by a factor so strong that it is in
effect a function of it. A \emph{deterministic view} is the limit: no
second variable, just a lookup. \emph{Promotion} replaces a deterministic
view by a second variable plus a soft factor tying it to the first, so
that hard factors can sit on the small new variable. A \emph{gate} is a
factor's statement about kinds it does not own: what the ground's texture
table says about a root tile. The principle adopted (\S\ref{sec:ledger}):
keep views as lookups; promote whatever carries hard factors across a
large domain; make the soft side a normalised conditional, not an
indicator penalty.

\paragraph{Exemplar coordinate.} A channel $u$ whose value is an index into
a painted \emph{exemplar}, a picture of the structure with a
\emph{mask}. Its view $\alpha(u)$ is the exemplar's statement at that
position: \code{FREE} (outside the mask: the cell belongs to whoever else
speaks), \code{EARTH} (the ring around the structure: any earth tile), or a
specific tile. \emph{Coherence} is the preference for a cell's coordinate
to be the shift of its neighbour's. Two \code{FREE} coordinates owe each
other nothing, so the energy minimum is a verbatim copy of the masked
region surrounded by free continuations.

\paragraph{Refinement.} A level's state is initialised as a consistent
refinement of the level above: every fine variable set to a value the
coarse state implies, before any fine sweep. The alternative, starting
blank, lets early hard commitments freeze against the coarse state
(\S\ref{sec:ledger}, trenches).

\paragraph{Schedule.} Levels run top-down, each designed channel written
once and each sampled channel swept a fixed number of times. No ramps, no
annealing, no global checks.

\section{Principles}
\label{sec:principles}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Three layers, three authors.} A channel's author publishes
  views. Whoever couples two channels writes tables over views. A kernel's
  author defines the kernel's input shape. The generic layer converts
  tables to shapes and stacks them; a channel author never sees a kernel,
  a kernel never sees a channel set. Only a computed factor (the
  certificate) crosses the line, and it is written once with the kernel.
\item \textbf{Hard factors live on small domains.} Support on the solid
  bit, the seam on ports, the certificate on mass and joins, the surface
  rule on windows. Where a hard rule would land on a large domain through
  a deterministic view, promote.
\item \textbf{Top-down only.} A coarser channel never reads a finer one.
  Honourability (every coarse state admits a fine realisation) is the
  coarse author's obligation; the surface channel is honourable under
  support by construction (bottom rows of a chunk solid).
\item \textbf{Start each level as a refinement of the one above.}
\item \textbf{Joint blocks where channels are tightly coupled.} If a
  move in one channel is only ever accepted together with a move in
  another, the kernel must draw both; separate kernels either lock or
  need calibration that does not exist.
\item \textbf{Measure violations, not vibes.} Every run reports hard-rule
  violations (support, certificate, loops), sites with no candidate,
  coordinate mismatches, and pruned cells; a mechanism is accepted when
  these are zero at the end of a fixed budget.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Factors in the final system}

\paragraph{Ground set} (\code{ground.factors}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{support}, hard pair on \code{solid}, offset $(1, 0)$: a solid
  tile needs a solid tile below; below the map counts as solid.
\item \emph{texture}, soft pairs on \code{ground} (sky, earth, rock, OTHER)
  horizontal and vertical, from a handful of contact and adjacency
  energies; OTHER is the \emph{wildcard}: against a neighbour of kind $x$
  it takes the $(x, x)$ entry, against sky the solid entry, and its unary
  is the mean of the solids'. This is the gate: a root tile is
  texture-neutral wherever it stands.
\item \emph{count honour}, soft count of \code{solid} against
  $8 \cdot \code{surf}$, quadratic.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Root set, structural} (\code{roots.factors(counted=False)}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{port seam}, pairs on \code{mE}/\code{mW} and \code{mS}/\code{mN}
  (0 not a root, the mass if that side has a port, BLANK for a root without
  one): a port facing a non-port costs \code{dangle} $= 1$. Soft on
  purpose: an unmatched port is how an unfinished copy looks mid-sweep.
\item \emph{contact}, hard pairs on \code{root}: a root never touches sky;
  a trunk has sky above and not below.
\item \emph{certificate}, tree mode, joins from the port views.
\end{itemize}
The counted tables of the first pass (unary over mass and degree
marginals, port-direction statistics, PMI of joined masses, crowding) are
off: the coordinate channel supplies the statistics.

\paragraph{Coordinate coupling} (\code{coord.coupling}). A pair on
\code{tile.self} $\times$ \code{u.alpha} at offset $(0, 0)$: $\nu = 6$ per
mismatch; FREE forbids root and trunk tiles softly, EARTH accepts any
earth tile. It is not added to the model's factor list; the joint kernel
applies it from its table, because the kernel draws $u$ and $t$ together.

\paragraph{Coarse window set} (\code{coord.Coarse.factors}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{coherence}, soft pairs on \code{ey} and \code{ex} with shift
  tables: $0$ if the neighbour is this window shifted by 8, $\lambda_8 = 1$
  otherwise between two windows, $f = 0.1$ per view beside FREE,
  $0$ for FREE--FREE.
\item \emph{edge compatibility}, hard pairs on signature views: two windows
  may abut iff the ports crossing the shared edge agree in position and
  mass with parent-ward flipped; a child crossing against FREE costs
  \code{cut} $= 2$; a parent crossing against FREE is $\infty$.
\item \emph{surface}, hard pairs against \code{surf} at $(0,0)$ and
  $(-1,0)$: every root cell of the window lies below the chunk's first
  solid row, a trunk sits on it, and a trunk on the window's top row needs
  an empty chunk above.
\item \emph{unary}: $-4$ on a trunk window, $-2$ on every other masked
  window.
\item \emph{certificate}, plain mode, joins = compatible and parent-ward,
  mass $1$ on every masked window, trunk windows the roots: no headless
  fragment.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Kernels}

\paragraph{Generic} (\code{kernel.sweep}). Used for the tile channel in the
first pass and for \code{u8} throughout. With a certificate it draws
$(t, d)$; the certificate's site logic is \code{site\_weights}, shared.

\paragraph{Joint coordinate--tile} (\code{coord.joint\_sweep}). Per site:
the tile energies from the model's factors, the certificate weights per
tile, the coordinate energies per candidate (coherence, plus a soft
parent-honour $\mu = 2$ against the refinement), the coupling table joining
them; one Gumbel-max over (candidate, tile) pairs, then $d$. Candidates: the
current value, the four coherent continuations, one random coordinate,
one coordinate whose alpha equals the current tile, and the parent's
refinement.

\subsection{Schedule}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \code{surf}: value noise over chunk columns, once.
\item \code{u8}, \code{d8}: 30 sweeps of the generic kernel ($\sim$1 s).
\item Refinement: tiles from \code{surf} (bottom rows solid); then for every
  placed window, the fine coordinates, tiles and certificate depth, with
  depth recomputed by breadth-first search over the stitched port tree
  from the trunk tiles, and root tiles not reached pruned to ground.
\item \code{tile}, \code{u}, \code{cert}: 30 joint sweeps ($0.18$ s each).
\end{enumerate}
Total about 8 s single-core for $128 \times 512$. Outcome: 9 trunks, $\sim$2200
root cells, 28 recombined seams, $0$ support / certificate / loop
violations, $0$ sites without a candidate, $0$ pruned, $\sim$20 coordinate
mismatches.

\subsection{Exemplars}

ASCII, parsed by \code{coord.parse\_exemplar}: \code{T} trunk, digits mass,
dot earth, space sky. Ports are derived as a breadth-first spanning tree
from the trunks over mass-non-increasing adjacencies, so a painted band
becomes a comb and the exemplar satisfies the tree certificate. The mask
is the roots plus a one-cell earth ring below the trunk row. The big
system ($40 \times 84$, 504 root cells) is rasterised from strokes by
\code{experiments/paint\_big.py}; the sheet is it and its mirror image.

\section{Ledger}
\label{sec:ledger}

\subsection{What worked as designed}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stacking.} Ground and roots were written against the generic
  layer separately and combined by concatenating factor lists on one tile
  domain. No kernel edit and no code beyond the script, in every
  combination.
\item \textbf{Views from tags.} The ground's support and count saw root
  tiles as solid, the roots' rules saw soil and stone as earth, with no
  cross-reference between the modules.
\item \textbf{The certificate.} Attachment, monotonicity and the one-parent
  rule held at zero violations in every run once implemented, at both
  levels, with the same code.
\item \textbf{Refinement.} Starting a level as a refinement of the one above
  removed the trunk shafts (8 cells deep to $\le 2$) and, with the coarse
  coordinate channel, turned 800 sweeps of sequential growth into 30 + 30
  sweeps at fixed budget, with four times the root cells.
\item \textbf{The generic kernel at level 8.} The window channel needed no
  new kernel: coherence, the surface rule, the bonuses and the certificate
  are tables and the existing computed factor, on a domain of 4000 values
  at 1024 sites.
\item \textbf{Metrics.} Every failure below was found by a number going
  the wrong way, not by looking at a picture.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What was painful, with the cause}

Ordered by time spent.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Energy balance between a copying channel and a texture
  channel: three gates.} Roots as earth in the ground view: a root cell
  inside fine-grained stone paid the lost rock cohesion and copies stalled
  at a third of their size. Roots transparent (zero row): a root cell lost
  the solid cohesion every solid gets, about $3$ per cell, and nothing
  grew; the trunk also lost against soil's cohesion when the dangling cost
  was $4$. The wildcard fixed it. Lesson: a gate is not ``say nothing'';
  it is ``say what you would say about any solid here''. The marginalised
  form is the right default.
\item \textbf{Label entropy versus soft coupling.} With 45 root kinds
  against one soil, an indicator coupling of $2$ between tile and
  coordinate was beaten by entropy ($44 e^{-2} > 1$), and raising it to $6$
  made the two-step path (coordinate then tile) too rare to grow. Fix: the
  joint kernel. Lesson: a derived channel with a large label domain must
  be drawn with its parent; and a soft side must be a normalised
  conditional, not a penalty.
\item \textbf{Nucleation through hard seams.} A port facing a non-port as
  $\infty$ means no single-site move can create a port pair; trunks were
  never placed. Fix: the dangling cost soft ($4$, later $1$). Lesson: a
  hard rule between two cells that are both sampled is a two-site move in
  disguise; either soften it or draw the pair.
\item \textbf{Thin wiry roots from pair statistics.} Pair tables cannot
  carry shape; with undirected cells attraction fills blobs (more good
  pairs in a blob than a chain). Port tiles with join bits fixed the blobs;
  only exemplar coordinates fixed the shape.
\item \textbf{The exemplar's own statistics as barriers.} Counting mass and
  degree jointly priced the thick tip every growing root passes through
  at $\sim 8$, because the painted exemplar had no thick tips. Counting
  marginals fixed it. Lesson: statistics counted from a finished picture
  describe finished states, and single-site Gibbs must pass through
  unfinished ones.
\item \textbf{Random proposals in uniform regions.} Eight random coordinate
  proposals per site turned thick bands into patchwork blobs, since every
  interior coordinate matches. One random proposal keeps the shape with
  some variation. The same ambiguity is why coarse recombination needs
  edge signatures rather than content similarity.
\item \textbf{The coarse join and the fine tree disagreeing.} Signature
  compatibility without direction let 43\% of placed root cells be pruned
  (a thin crossing led into a thick band, or into a piece whose parent was
  cut); with direction but symmetric witnesses, 32\%. Direction plus
  parent-ward witnesses plus ``a parent crossing is never cut'' brought it
  to zero, at the price of 100 coarse sites with no legal move: the hard
  inter-block rule locks chunks, as predicted for deterministic couplings.
\item \textbf{The certificate's $d$ range.} With $\delta = 0.1$ and
  $D_{\max} = 64$ the geometric draw spread $d$ by $\sim 10$ per step and
  chains hit the cap at depth 6. The product formula above gave the rule
  ($\delta \cdot \text{depth} < 1$, $D_{\max} \gg \text{depth}/\delta$).
\item \textbf{Trenches.} Starting the tile level blank let a trunk be
  placed at the bottom row of its chunk while the ground was still rising,
  and support plus sky-above froze a shaft. Refinement fixed it.
\item \textbf{A shrinkage with no single cause.} Before the coarse channel,
  copies in the ground saturated at 40\% of their size alone; texture,
  clipping, flat terrain, support alone and count alone were each ruled
  out, and the cause was never isolated. The coarse channel made the
  question moot. It remains the clearest example of an interaction that
  per-factor reasoning does not reveal.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pros and cons, stated plainly}

\paragraph{Pros.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Adding a channel set is a small, local piece of code, and
  combination is concatenation.
\item Hard rules are exact and cheap, and they are what carried every
  result: no run ended with a violation.
\item One computed factor, the certificate, covered attachment at the tile
  level and no-headless-fragments at the chunk level with the same code.
\item The fixed budget is real once a parent level exists: 60 sweeps, 8 s,
  for a $128 \times 512$ world with nine root systems.
\item The failure modes are measurable and were measured.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cons.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Calibration between channel sets is manual, was most of the work, and
  grows with the number of pairs. The framework does not predict energy
  balance; it only measures it.
\item The kernel changed five times for new hard structure (join bits,
  interval draw, tree mode, joint block, explicit joins at level 8). The
  ``kernel is stable, channels plug in'' claim holds for tables, not for
  computed factors.
\item Deterministic couplings lock the sampler (coarse parent cuts, 100
  stuck chunks); soft couplings lock the designer (every soft knob above).
  The promotion with a normalised conditional that would resolve this is
  designed, not built.
\item Diversity is limited by the sheet: with one system and its mirror
  only 28 seams recombine. The mechanism waits on painted content.
\item The ground texture is salt-and-pepper at these energies; it was never
  tuned because it was not the question.
\item Speed is single-core numba, and the joint kernel enumerates every
  tile per candidate: $0.18$ s per fine sweep is fine for a demo, not for
  a world.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What the next pass should change first}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Promote the edge signature to a channel with a smoothed
  $-\log p(v \mid u)$ coupling, hard seams between promises, hard honour
  below; measure whether near-matching edges stitch and whether the 100
  stuck chunks free up.
\item A sheet of several painted systems, to give recombination something
  to recombine.
\item Fuzzy matching at level 8 only (the PCA patch energy of
  \code{generic.py} on the alpha image, $\sim$2--3 s added).
\item Built-in ablation and per-factor energy breakdown, so that the
  shrinkage-with-no-single-cause class of failure is found by the tool.
\end{enumerate}
