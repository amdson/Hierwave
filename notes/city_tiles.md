# City tiles for WFC

A macro-scale 2D city tile set that tiles the plane trivially but that plain
WFC cannot finish on a large patch, and that looks like a city only with a
coarse plan. Rules and generator: `castlegen/citytiles.py`; exported tiles
(corners, edges, weight): `castlegen/citytiles.json`; tests:
`tests/test_citytiles.py`.

## Model
Dual-grid Wang tiles. A tile's four corners are city blocks with a zone
(P park, R residential, C commercial, D downtown, I industrial, W water, and
position-indexed airport labels for a 2x3 or 3x2 block footprint); its four
edges carry what crosses them centre to centre (none, street, avenue, rail,
highway). The tile centre is an intersection. Neighbours match on the two
shared corners and the shared edge feature. The set is every tuple that passes
the rules: 3907 tiles, 665 shapes up to rotation and mirror
(`images/city_tile_catalogue.png`).

Rules, all inside one tile:
- topology: streets of degree 2-4 (dead end only inside housing), avenues
  and rail straight or turning (a straight one takes street or level
  crossings), highways straight only (crossed straight through: overpass,
  interchange);
- zoning: at most two land zones per tile (three districts never meet at a
  crossing), compatible pairs only (D-C-R gradient, I never with R or D,
  airport with I, C, P, W);
- districts: a boundary between two land zones carries a road;
- nuisance: no housing on a rail or highway tile;
- shores carry nothing (waterfront blocks are served from land), and a road
  that meets water bridges it straight, as the tile's only feature;
- airport footprints exact and closed, nothing crosses the airfield, a road
  on every land perimeter edge.

Easy infinite tilings: all park, all water, one 4-way-street tile of R, C, D,
or a highway grid of I (each tiles the plane alone; tested).

Weights (what a sampler sees by default): per corner zone, per edge feature,
x0.12 per edge whose corners change zone (districts, not speckle), x0.1 per
bridge, x0.15 for an all-built tile with no road (superblocks rare).

## Plain WFC fails; guidance helps
`notes/experiments/city_wfc.py`: min-entropy WFC, arc-consistent propagation
through sockets, open borders, 12 seeds per cell of the table. *repair*: on a
contradiction un-collapse a square of radius 2 (growing with repeated
failures nearby) and resume, at most 50 times. *plan*: a soft coarse map
(ordered bands D|C|R|P|I on the level sets of one smooth field of scale 12,
plus a river) multiplies a tile's weight by e^1.5 per corner agreeing with it.

| size | plain | plain + repair | plan + repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32² | 12/12 | 12/12, 0 repairs | 12/12, 0 repairs |
| 64² | 5/12 | 12/12, 5.2 repairs/run | 12/12, 6.0 |
| 96² | 0/12 | 7/12, 32.1 (cap 50) | 10/12, 11.5 |

Plain WFC's completion falls with area, so it cannot fill an infinite world.
Its contradictions are local dead ends that arc consistency cannot see two
steps ahead: avenues, rail and district fronts meeting with no tile to join
them, three districts converging, bridges reaching a shore that is not
straight. Repair alone keeps up only on small patches and leaves no city: one
giant district, a speckled sea, airports floating in water
(`images/city_wfc_plain_repair.png`). With the plan the map has a downtown
inside a commercial ring, housing, industry and a river, and at 96² it needs
about a third of the repairs (`images/city_wfc_plan_repair.png`). The plan
carries what the rules cannot: districts at a scale of ~10 tiles, arranged
so that neighbours are compatible and three never meet.

## Knobs
- Harder: drop `BOUNDARY_W` (more coherent districts collide more), forbid
  avenue crossings, add footprints.
- Fewer tiles: drop the V airport (it mirrors H) or the D zone.
- The JSON is regenerated by `uv run python -m castlegen.citytiles`.
- Water still pools a little under repair: it is the least constrained
  zone. `ZONE_W["W"]` and `BRIDGE_W` set how much.
